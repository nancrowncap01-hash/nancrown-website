# Custom Hat Samples: Timing and What to Check

A custom hat sample from NanCrown typically takes 7–15 days to make, depending on the style and requirements, once the artwork and fabric are confirmed. Shipping is additional, and revisions are included in the 7–15 day sampling window. When it arrives, check the fit, shape, materials, logo, stitching and closure against your agreed specifications before approving it for production.

## What the sample timeline covers

The sampling period is for making the physical sample, not a promise that it will reach your door within that period. Confirming artwork and fabric gives the factory a clear starting point; shipping comes afterward. If a feature needs a change, ask how that affects the schedule before setting a launch date.

A useful timeline works backward from when you need finished hats. Leave room for the sample to travel and a planning buffer for the people making the decision to review it. Bulk production normally takes 25–30 days after the pre-production sample is approved and the agreed payment is received; shipping is additional. Confirm the schedule for your particular design and order with the factory.

A standard physical sample with a single logo costs US$50, excluding shipping. Additional logo positions and complex designs are quoted separately. For current details, see [pricing and lead times](/pricing).

## Compare the sample with the agreed brief

Put the sample next to your latest approved artwork, measurements and material notes. A physical hat can look different from a flat mockup because fabric bends, seams shape the crown and decoration sits on a curved surface. The question is not simply whether the hat looks good on its own, but whether it matches the product you intend to sell or hand out.

Check the front, sides, back and inside in good light. Photograph anything you want to discuss, including a wider view that shows where the issue is on the hat. If a measurement matters, include the measuring tape and explain where you started and stopped. That context gives the factory something more useful than “make it a little bigger.”

## Check fit and shape on a head

Try the hat on, rather than judging the crown while it sits on a table. Look at how high the crown sits, how the side panels follow the head, and whether the brim has the curve and length you expected. Open and close an adjustable strap through its usable range and check that it holds comfortably without leaving an awkward tail.

If different people will wear the hats, ask people with different head sizes to try the sample. Note where it feels tight or loose and which adjustment setting they used. A cap can match a drawing yet still feel too shallow or too deep in use. Share specific fit observations and the measurements you want changed; don't assume that changing the closure alone will fix the crown.

## Inspect the fabric and color

Feel the outer fabric and any mesh, lining or sweatband included in your design. Check whether the weight, texture and stiffness suit the intended use. A soft crown will drape differently from a structured one, even when the artwork is identical. Look at the sample in natural light as well as indoor light, since lighting can change your perception of color.

Compare colors with the references agreed for the order, and identify the material being discussed: fabric, thread and a patch can reflect light differently. If a color seems wrong, send a clear photo and ask the factory to review it against the agreed reference. Screen images alone are a poor way to settle a close color match.

## Review the logo in its actual position

Check the logo's size, placement, orientation and colors against the approved artwork. View it straight on and at an angle, then look closely at small lettering and fine details. On a curved crown, a logo that looks centered in a digital file may appear different once worn. Check whether it sits where you intended relative to the seams and brim.

For embroidery, look for legibility, coverage and loose threads. For a patch, inspect the patch surface and the way its edges are attached. If the design is difficult to read, identify the particular line or letter rather than asking for a vague improvement. The factory can then discuss whether the artwork, size or placement should change. Avoid approving a logo solely from a close-up photo: its relationship to the whole hat matters too.

## Look inside and test the construction

Turn the hat over and inspect seams, stitching, sweatband, labels and closure. Seams should sit neatly, and the brim should feel securely joined to the crown. Open and close the fastener several times, checking for comfortable operation and any sharp or irritating edges. Look for loose threads or puckering around decoration and seams.

A sample is also where practical details become visible: an inside label may rub, a closure may be hard to adjust, or a brim may feel different from what the buyer expected. These observations are more actionable when you explain the intended use. A hat for a run, for example, needs to stay comfortable during movement; a retail sample also needs to represent the finish your customer will see.

## Send a clear decision before production

Collect feedback from the people who need to approve the product and send it together. Separate changes that are essential from preferences, and attach annotated photos or measurements. Conflicting comments sent in different messages can lead to rework or an unclear final specification.

If changes are needed, ask the factory to confirm the revised details and whether another physical sample is appropriate. If the sample is right, give a clear written approval and keep a record of the approved specifications and photos. The [sample approval guide](/solutions/sample-approval) can help you organize that decision. Approval should reflect the actual product you want produced, including details that may not show in a mockup.

## FAQ

### Does the sample time include delivery?

No. The stated sampling period covers making the sample; allow additional time for shipping and confirm delivery arrangements for your destination.

### Should I approve a sample from photos alone?

Photos can help you discuss visible details, but handling and wearing a physical sample lets you assess fit, fabric and closure. Decide with the factory what review is appropriate for your order.

### What if the logo or fit is wrong?

Send specific feedback with annotated photos or measurements. Confirm the proposed changes, their effect on the schedule and whether you need to review another physical sample before approval.

### Does approving the sample mean the order ships immediately?

No. Bulk production follows sample approval and receipt of the agreed payment, and shipping takes additional time. Confirm the complete schedule for your order.
