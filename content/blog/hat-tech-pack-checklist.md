# What to Include in a Hat Tech Pack

A hat tech pack should describe the shape, measurements, materials, colors, logo artwork, labels and closure clearly enough for a factory to understand the product you want to make. Include drawings or reference photos, a quantity breakdown and any details you still need help choosing. It does not need to be a polished technical document: a clear, consistent brief gives the factory a useful starting point for a quote and sample.

## Start with the product and its purpose

Name the hat style and describe who will wear it and where. A cap intended for a running club may need a different feel from a structured cap for a streetwear collection. Explain whether the priority is a soft fit, a defined crown, a lightweight feel or a particular look. If you have a physical hat that captures the shape you want, say which features are relevant and which should change.

Add front, side, back and inside views if you can. Simple sketches with notes are more useful than a single attractive image that hides the closure or side profile. Label each view so the factory can tell which feature you mean. A reference photo can communicate a direction, but it should not be the only instruction for a detail that matters to your brand.

## Describe the shape and construction

Record the crown profile and whether you want it structured or soft. Describe the brim shape and length, the panel arrangement and any visible seam details. Note where you want ventilation openings and whether their appearance matters. If your design uses different materials on the front and back, mark the boundary on the drawing.

Avoid relying on words such as “classic” or “normal.” Those terms can mean different things to different people. Instead, point to a reference and explain what you like about it: for example, a lower crown that sits close to the head or a curved brim with a particular profile. If you are unsure how to specify a feature, mark it as a question for the factory rather than guessing.

## Add measurements and sizing

List the measurements that define your intended fit, such as head circumference, crown depth and brim length. State the units and show on the drawing where each measurement starts and ends. For an adjustable cap, identify the intended size range and closure; for a fitted style, provide the sizes you need.

Measurements taken from a favorite hat are useful only if the measuring method is clear. A crown measured along its surface can give a different result from a vertical depth measurement. Include a photo of the tape in position if needed. Ask the factory to confirm how it will measure the sample and discuss acceptable variation before you approve the specification.

## Specify materials, colors and hardware

Identify the fabric for each part of the hat, including any mesh, lining and sweatband. Describe the desired texture, weight and feel when you know them. If you do not know a fabric's technical name, explain its intended use and send a clear reference; the factory can discuss suitable options with you.

Give a color reference for each material and for the stitching, closure and decoration where those colors matter. Label the color on the relevant drawing rather than leaving the factory to infer it from a screen image. Fabric, thread and hardware can look different under the same light, so ask how the proposed materials will be checked against your reference.

Name the closure and describe its material, color and finish. A photo of the back view helps show how you expect the strap and fastener to sit. If the exact hardware is flexible, say so; if its appearance is essential, make that clear before the sample is made.

## Prepare artwork for each logo position

Provide the cleanest editable artwork you have and identify the intended decoration method, such as embroidery or a patch. Mark the size, position and orientation of every logo on the appropriate view. Include artwork colors and any lettering that must remain readable. Separate artwork files from inspiration images so the factory knows which design is intended for production.

Think about how the design will sit on a curved surface and near seams. Very fine lines or small lettering may need adjustment for the chosen method or size. Ask the factory to review the artwork for suitability before sampling, and agree on any changes rather than assuming the digital image will translate exactly onto fabric.

## Show labels, packaging and order details

Draw or describe any inside label, hangtag or packaging you want, including the text, placement and color. Supply final label artwork and check spelling carefully. If you have requirements for information that must appear on a label or packaging, share them with the factory and check the rules for the market where the hats will be sold.

Give the intended quantity by style, color and size, along with your destination and required delivery date. Mention whether the order is for retail, an event or team use, and flag any details that cannot change. This context helps the factory discuss the specification and prepare a relevant quote. For a broader introduction to the process, see [starting a hat brand](/start-a-hat-brand).

## Keep the brief usable through sampling

Put the latest drawings, artwork and notes together, give the document a version and date, and identify the person who can approve changes. Conflicting files are a common source of avoidable rework: a revised logo in one attachment and an older size in another can leave the factory guessing. Remove superseded instructions or mark them clearly as obsolete.

When a sample arrives, compare it with the current brief and gather feedback in one place. Use annotated photos and measurements to explain changes. Confirm the updated specification with the factory and retain the approved version for production. A tech pack is a working reference: it becomes more useful as open questions are resolved, not just when the first file is sent.

## FAQ

### Do I need professional drawings to start?

No. Clear sketches, labeled views and reference photos can start the conversation. Ask the factory for help with details you cannot yet specify.

### What if I do not know the fabric name?

Describe how the hat should feel and where it will be worn, then share a reference. Ask the factory to discuss suitable materials before you finalize the brief.

### Can I use a photo instead of measurements?

A photo is useful for communicating appearance, but it may not establish fit or scale. Add measurements and explain how they were taken whenever those details matter.

### Should I update the tech pack after sampling?

Yes. Record agreed changes and keep the approved artwork and specifications together so the production reference reflects the final decision.
